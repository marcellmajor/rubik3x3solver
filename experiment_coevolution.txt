Old solver instrumentation rules:
  - toolbox.evaluate is wrapped: a budget check runs before each evaluation, then the original evaluator runs unchanged (including its global min_moves/best_fitness_ever updates).
  - rotate() is wrapped: a budget check runs before every face turn, so the time limit can stop the script inside an evaluation instead of after a whole population; it also counts calls and quarter turns.
  - manhattan_distance() and misplaced_stickers() are wrapped only to count calls.
  - After each completed evaluation the harness replays the individual's genes from the original cube with solver_deap_coevolution's reducer and best-prefix normalisation (capacity 32), verifies the chosen prefix by independent replay and keeps it if its (unsolved, H, length) rank is strictly better. This also covers partially completed generations before the hall-of-fame update.
  - toolbox.select is wrapped only to mark generation boundaries and to count distinct best-prefix endpoints of each fully evaluated population; it passes the arguments through unchanged.
  - Observation never touches the global random module, the individuals or their fitness values, so selection decisions are unchanged (checked by the neutrality run below).
  - Observation time is paused out of the baseline's search budget and reported separately, so harness overhead does not shorten the baseline's own 120 s of search.
  - A verified feasible solve stops the run early.

7. Remaining differences between the two harnesses
-------------------------------------------------
- The old solver keeps its own fitness: min over prefixes of manhattan + 0*misplaced + length/MAX_GENES, seeded by the module-global min_moves/best_fitness_ever, which change during the run. The new solver ranks (unsolved, H, reduced length) with a pure H. Only the replayed endpoint cost/solved status is compared.
- The old evaluator applies all 32 genes and computes misplaced_stickers at every prefix even though its weight is 0; the new solver normalises to the best reduced prefix and caches pure H values.
- The old GA uses the module-global random generator; the new solver uses one seeded random.Random per run. Equal seed numbers do not imply comparable random streams.
- Old-solver harness observation (best-prefix replay) is paused out of its budget; the new solver's own normalisation and verification are part of its search and count against its budget.
- The old solver still prints its logbook every generation and print_best every 25 generations (into a log sink); the new solver reports every 100 generations.
